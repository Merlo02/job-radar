<td>
<p>Connecting urban systems, human behaviour, and well-being: How can cities reduce resource use and carbon emissions while maintaining or improving human well-being? How do changes in urban provisioning systems influence citizens’ perceptions, behaviours, and everyday choices? And how do these individual changes translate into changes in resource demand and material and energy flows across urban systems?</p>
<p> </p>
<p>The Laboratory for Human-Environment Relations in Urban Systems (HERUS) at the School of Architecture, Civil and Environmental Engineering (ENAC), EPFL, investigates how urban systems transform towards sustainability. A key challenge is understanding how changes in urban infrastructure and provisioning systems can be aligned with low-carbon futures, social acceptance, and human well-being.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>Within a planned interdisciplinary research project in collaboration with the University of Freiburg im Breisgau, Germany, we investigate how transformations of urban provisioning systems can contribute to low-carbon futures and improved human well-being at the city scale.</p>
<p> </p>
<p><br>The PhD project will examine how urban planning interventions and changes in provisioning systems influence citizens’ perceptions, behavioural choices, and subjective well-being. A central aim is to connect these social and behavioural dimensions with socio-metabolic research, investigating how individual behaviours translate into changes in resource demand and material and energy flows across urban systems.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>The successful candidate will work in close collaboration with our partners at the University of Freiburg, as well as with the HERUS research team. </p>

<p>The project involves refining optics configurations, implementing new tuning knobs, and developing correction schemes to enhance luminosity, beam lifetime, and dynamic aperture—critical figures of merit for collider optimization. <p>The project has three main ambitions: (1) developing methods to measure verticality and its impacts in data-scarce contexts, in Africa and elsewhere; (2) documenting, theoretically and empirically, the interactions between vertical growth, capitalism, governance, inequalities and everyday life; (3) co-designing more sustainable and inclusive models for the future of African cities.</p>
<p> </p>
<p>The research is organised into four work packages. The postdoctoral researcher will contribute to measuring urban verticality and its environmental impacts (WP1), while providing scientific coordination for the geospatial, modelling and digital observatory components of the project (WP4).</p></div></div><div style="padding:10.0px 0.0px;border:1.0px solid transparent"><div style="font-size:16.0px;word-wrap:break-word"><H2 style="font-size:1.0em;margin:0.0px">Research Focus</H2></div><div><p>How can verticalisation and its environmental consequences be measured, modelled and projected in data-scarce African cities, and how can these results be translated into an open and reproducible digital observatory supporting research, planning and participatory exploration of future urban scenarios?</p>

Linking all datasets will allow to map both short – and long-range interactions and collective effects pertaining to all molecular actors. This allows to solve the puzzle surrounding concentrated electrolyte solutions and how they work, for example in their charge transmitting/storing functions and lead to a better understanding, and ultimately new technology.</p></div></div><div style="padding:10.0px 0.0px;border:1.0px solid transparent"><div style="font-size:16.0px;word-wrap:break-word"><H2 style="font-size:1.0em;margin:0.0px"><b>Main duties and responsibilities</b></H2></div><div><p>As a PhD Student, you will be expected to:</p>
